---
title: Pricing by formula
description: "Price a job from a formula of its quantity, size and page count, for prices that follow a curve rather than tiers: custom-size signs, per-item minimums, size bands and more."
---

Some prices don't come in tiers. A sign shop's quote is often a curve: the
first sign carries the setup, each further sign costs less, and every number
depends on the sign's size. You can approximate that with quantity breaks,
but you need a row for every quantity, and a product the customer sizes
themselves can't be broken down by size *and* quantity at the same time.

A **Formula** price rule calculates the price directly from the job:

```text
P1 + F * (qty - 1) + b * (1 - 0.25^(qty - 1)) / 0.75
```

You write the formula once, in the builder, and the configurator works the
price out for whatever size and quantity the customer enters.

<Note>
    Formula pricing is available on Shopify, and on WooCommerce from plugin
    version 0.4.16.
</Note>

## When to use a formula

Reach for a formula when:

- your price list is already a spreadsheet formula,
- the price depends on the size **and** the quantity together, as with
  custom-size signs, banners and panels,
- each item has a minimum price, whatever its size, or
- you price in size bands *and* want quantity to count as well.

For simpler cases the other tools are quicker to set up: a per-unit price
with [quantity breaks](/print-options/guides/pricing#quantity-breaks), a
[price matrix](/print-options/guides/pricing#price-matrix-an-exact-price-per-combination)
when your supplier quotes every combination, or
[area pricing](/print-options/guides/pricing#area-pricing) at a flat rate per
square metre.

## How a formula rule works

A formula is a price rule like any other, so it lives in the same places:

- **On a field** (its **Pricing** tab): it applies while that field is
  shown. A formula on the size field is the usual choice.
- **On a choice** (open the choice's details, then **Pricing**): it applies
  while that choice is selected. Give each material its own formula when
  aluminium and foam board are priced differently.

The formula's result is the rule's **whole charge for the order**. Unlike a
per-unit price, it is *not* multiplied by the quantity afterwards. Use `qty`
in the formula wherever the quantity matters.

Everything else in your option set still applies around it: the base price
is added, a choice's percentage (a rush surcharge, say) applies to the
running total after it, and setup fees and the minimum price come last.
To let the formula be the whole price, set the base price to **0, once per
order**.

## Your first formula: custom-size signs

This walk-through builds the sign price from the top of this page. Each sign
size has three numbers, all worked out from the sign's area in square feet:

| Name | Meaning | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `P1` | The price of the first sign | `25 + 6 * area` |
| `F` | Each further sign, in the long run | `4 + 3.5 * area` |
| `b` | The extra on the second sign, quartering with every sign after it | `8 + 2 * area` |

1.  **Add the size.** Add a **Dimensions** field labelled *Size*, with
    inches as its unit. (The **Posters** template already has one, in
    centimetres.)
2.  **Zero the base price.** Under **Product** settings, set the base
    price to **0**, charged **Once per order**.
3.  **Add the rule.** Select the *Size* field, open its **Pricing** tab,
    click **+ Add price rule**, and change the rule's type to **Formula**.
    A starter formula, `10 * area * qty`, appears.
4.  **Set the units.** **Size from** is the field the formula reads (*Size*).
    Set **w and h in** to **in** and **area in** to **ft²**. The builder
    picks these for you from the size field: inches go with square feet,
    centimetres with square metres.
5.  **Add the steps.** Click **+ Add step** three times, and fill in the
    names and formulas from the table above: `P1` = `25 + 6 * area`,
    `F` = `4 + 3.5 * area`, `b` = `8 + 2 * area`.
6.  **Write the price.** In **Price =**, enter
    `P1 + F * (qty - 1) + b * (1 - 0.25^(qty - 1)) / 0.75`.
7.  **Check it.** Open **Check prices**, enter a size such as 24 × 18, and
    compare the table with your own price list (see
    [Checking your prices](#checking-your-prices)).
8.  **Publish.**

For three sign sizes, that formula charges:

| Size | Area | 1 sign | 2 signs | 5 signs | 10 signs | 25 signs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12 × 12 in | 1 ft² | $31.00 | $48.50 | $74.28 | $111.83 | $224.33 |
| 24 × 18 in | 3 ft² | $43.00 | $71.50 | $119.59 | $192.17 | $409.67 |
| 36 × 24 in | 6 ft² | $61.00 | $106.00 | $187.56 | $312.67 | $687.67 |

<Tip>
    Steps are optional. You could write the whole price as one long formula,
    but naming the parts the way your price list does (first sign, further
    signs, premium) makes the formula readable months later, and an error
    points at the step it is in.
</Tip>

## Writing formulas

### Names you can use

| Name | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `qty` | The quantity being ordered. With a [quantity per option](/print-options/guides/quantities#quantity-per-option-size-runs) field, the total across all options. |
| `w`, `h` | The width and height, in the unit set under **w and h in**. |
| `area` | Width × height, in the unit set under **area in**. |
| `pages` | The uploaded file's page count. Needs a file upload that reports it, such as [Filecheck](/print-options/guides/uploads#filecheck-validated-uploads). |
| Your steps | Each step can use the steps above it, and the price can use them all. |

- Names don't care about capitals: `Qty`, `QTY` and `qty` are the same.
- `w`, `h` and `area` are 0 until the customer has entered a size. If the
  rule's **Size from** is set to **Uploaded file canvas**, they come from the
  artwork's own size instead of a dimensions field.

### Operators

| Write | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `+` `-` `*` `/` | Add, subtract, multiply, divide |
| `^` | Power: `0.25^(qty - 1)`, `area^0.5` |
| `( )` | Brackets, as deep as you need |
| `0.25` | Numbers take a **dot** for decimals, never a comma |

Two things a spreadsheet lets you do that a formula doesn't:

- **Write the multiplication.** `2 * area`, not `2area`.
- **Bracket a negative power.** Spreadsheets read `-2^2` as 4, maths reads
  it as −4. To avoid surprises the builder asks you to write `(-2)^2` or
  `-(2^2)`. `2^-1` (a negative exponent) is fine as it is.

### Functions

| Function | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `min(a, b, …)` | The smallest value | `min(qty, 100)` |
| `max(a, b, …)` | The largest value | `max(30, 12.99 * area)` |
| `round(x)`, `round(x, d)` | Rounds half up, to `d` decimals (0 to 4) | `round(9.5 * area, 2)` |
| `ceil(x)` | Rounds up to a whole number | `ceil(area)`: per started square foot |
| `floor(x)` | Rounds down to a whole number | `floor(qty / 10)` |
| `abs(x)` | Drops the sign | `abs(w - h)` |
| `sqrt(x)` | Square root | `sqrt(area)` |
| `band(x, upTo1, v1, upTo2, v2, …, last)` | A size or quantity table inside a formula | `band(area, 2, 30, 6, 26, 22)` |

`band()` works like a price-break table: it checks the limits from left to
right and gives the value of the first band that `x` fits under (each limit is
included in its band), or the last value when `x` is above them all.
`band(area, 2, 30, 6, 26, 22)` is 30 up to 2 ft², 26 up to 6 ft², and 22
above that.

### From a spreadsheet

Pasting a formula from a spreadsheet or a document works: the builder turns
`×` into `*` and `−` or `–` into `-` as you paste. Spreadsheet functions
translate like this:

| Spreadsheet | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| `POWER(a, b)` | `a^b` |
| `ROUNDUP(x, 0)` | `ceil(x)` |
| `ROUNDDOWN(x, 0)` | `floor(x)` |
| `ROUND(x, 2)` | `round(x, 2)` |
| `MIN(…)`, `MAX(…)` | `min(…)`, `max(…)` |
| `IF(area<=2, 30, IF(area<=6, 26, 22))` | `band(area, 2, 30, 6, 26, 22)` |
| `B2`, `C2` (cell references) | `qty`, `area`, or a step you name |
| `0,75` | `0.75` |

### Limits

- Up to **10 steps**, and up to **500 characters** per step or price.
- A step name starts with a letter and then uses letters, digits or `_`
  (up to 32 characters). It can't reuse a name the formula already has, such
  as `area`, `qty` or `max`.
- Only letters, digits, spaces and `_ . , + - * / ^ ( )` are allowed.
  There are no comparisons (`<`, `>`) and no `if`: use `min`, `max` and
  `band` instead. (WordPress would rewrite `<`, `>` and `%` when your option
  set is saved, so they are kept out of formulas on every platform.)
- A formula rule has no volume breaks of its own. Put the breaks in the
  formula with `band()`.
- A formula can't go on one option of a
  [quantity per option](/print-options/guides/quantities#quantity-per-option-size-runs)
  field. Put it on the field itself, where `qty` is the total.

## Units

**w and h in** and **area in** are separate on purpose. A US sign shop
measures in inches and prices by the square foot: with **in** and **ft²**, a
24 × 18 in sign reads as `w = 24`, `h = 18` and `area = 3`. The customer can
enter the size in any unit the size field offers. The formula always sees it
converted into your units.

| w and h in | area in |
| --- | --- |
| in, ft, cm, m | ft², in², m² |

Sizes are converted exactly as you would expect on paper. A 12 in width is
12 in the formula, not 11.999…, so `floor(w)` and `band()` limits behave.

## Checking your prices

**Check prices**, under the formula, shows what the rule charges at one size
for several quantities: the quantity field's presets if it has some, otherwise
1, 2, 5, 10 and 25. **This rule adds** is the rule's total; **Each** is that
divided by the quantity. It uses the same calculation as your storefront.

The table shows this rule only. Other rules (a material surcharge, a rush
percentage, a setup fee, the minimum price) still apply on top, and the
**Preview** pane on the right shows the whole product as your customers will
see it.

### When the builder flags a formula

Mistakes show in red under the step or price they are in, as you type, and
the option set can't be published until they are fixed:

| You see | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Use a dot for decimals: 0.75. | Write `0.75`, not `0,75`. |
| Missing an operator between 2 and area: write 2 \* area. | Add the `*`. |
| Add brackets: -(2^2) or (-2)^2. | Say which you mean. |
| Unknown name "aera". You can use: qty, w, h, area, pages. | A typo, or a step used before it is defined. The list shows what is available at that point. |
| "b" is worked out in a later step — move that step up. | Steps run top to bottom. |
| There is no function "pow". You can use: min, max, … | Use the operator instead: `a^b`. |
| max() needs at least two values, like max(a, b). | Add the missing value. |
| The limits in band() must increase (2 after 6). | List the bands smallest first. |
| A bracket opened here is never closed. | Add the missing `)`. |
| `"<"` can't be used in a formula. | Rewrite it with `min`, `max` or `band`. |

## When a formula has no price

Some formulas have no answer for some jobs: dividing by `qty - 1` when one
sign is ordered, or `sqrt` of a negative number. The configurator then tells
the customer *This size can't be priced online. Please contact us.* and won't
add the job to the cart. A job is never sold at a price of 0 because a formula
broke.

To keep every size priceable, give the size field a minimum and maximum
width and height, and guard any division, for example
`100 / max(qty - 1, 1)`.

## Recipes

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="A minimum price per item">
    A per-area price undercharges small items. This charges $12.99 per square
    foot, but never less than $30 a sign:

    ```text
    qty * max(30, 12.99 * area)
    ```

    Ten 9 × 12 in signs (0.75 ft² each) cost $300 rather than $97.40.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Size bands">
    $30 a sign up to 2 ft², $26 up to 6 ft², $22 above:

    ```text
    band(area, 2, 30, 6, 26, 22) * qty
    ```
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Per started square foot">
    Round the area up before pricing it, so 2.25 ft² is billed as 3:

    ```text
    ceil(area) * 9.5 * qty
    ```
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Edge finishing by the perimeter">
    Hemming or edge banding is priced by the length around the item. With
    **w and h in** set to **ft**:

    ```text
    2 * (w + h) * 0.4 * qty
    ```
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A discount that grows with quantity">
    0.1% off per item ordered, capped at 30%:

    ```text
    12 * qty * (1 - min(0.3, 0.001 * qty))
    ```
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Setup plus run">
    A $35 setup and $0.80 per item, as one rule:

    ```text
    35 + 0.8 * qty
    ```

    (A setup fee under **Product** settings does the same for the setup part,
    and is never scaled by percentages or multipliers.)
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Booklets by the sheet">
    Four pages fit on one sheet, and a started sheet is a whole sheet. With a
    file upload that reports the page count:

    ```text
    ceil(pages / 4) * 0.12 * qty
    ```
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A different formula for each material">
    Put a formula rule on each material choice instead of on the size field:
    only the selected material's formula applies.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

## Questions

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Can a formula use other fields' answers?">
    Not yet: a formula reads the quantity, the size and the page count. For
    choices such as material or finish, put a formula (or a percentage) on
    each choice instead.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a customer change the price the formula gives?">
    No. The configurator shows a preview, and the same formula runs again on
    our servers when the job is added to the cart. Your store charges the
    price our servers calculate and sign, as with every other price rule.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The storefront total differs from Check prices. Why?">
    **Check prices** shows the formula rule on its own. The storefront adds
    the base price, other price rules on the selected choices, percentages,
    setup fees and the minimum price. Set the base price to 0 if the formula
    should be the whole price.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does the 'Add 15 more to get 15% off' hint work with formulas?">
    The hint reads quantity breaks, so a product priced only by a formula
    doesn't show it. See
    [the next price break](/print-options/guides/quantities#show-the-next-price-break).
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
