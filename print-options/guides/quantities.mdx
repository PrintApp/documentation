The quantity is the number most of your pricing turns on: per-unit prices,
quantity breaks and formulas all read it, and it travels to the cart with
the job. Print Options gives you three ways to ask for it.

| You want | Use |
| --- | --- |
| One number: 250 flyers, 10 banners | A **Quantity** field |
| A count for each option: M × 5, L × 3, 3XL × 2 | A **Quantity per option** field |
| Different quantities depending on an earlier choice | Several **Quantity** fields that [take turns](#quantity-fields-that-take-turns) |

## The quantity field

Add a field of type **Quantity** and choose how it looks under **Display**:

- **Stepper**: − and + buttons around the number.
- **Preset pills** or **Preset dropdown**: only the quantities you list,
  such as 100 / 250 / 500 / 1000. Ideal for print runs.
- **Plain input**: a number box.

Its **Basics** tab has:

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Min**, **Max**, **Step**, **Default** | The range and grid of quantities a customer can order. |
| **Presets** | Comma-separated quantities for the pill and dropdown displays. |
| **Unit** | An optional word shown after the number in the cart and on the order: *5 banners*, *500 cards*. |
| **Show 'add N more' hint** | Tells the customer how close the next price break is. See [below](#show-the-next-price-break). |

A quantity is always one the customer can actually order: a number typed
between steps is moved onto the step grid (with a step of 25, 333 becomes 325)
and kept within the minimum and maximum. The cart line always shows the
quantity the job is charged for.

<Note>
    If nothing in your pricing depends on the quantity, the builder warns that
    *changing the quantity does not change the price*. See
    [The base price](/print-options/guides/pricing#the-base-price).
</Note>

## Show the next price break

Turn on **Show 'add N more' hint** on the quantity field, and the summary
tells customers how many more items reach the next quantity break:

- *Add 15 more to get 15% off*, when the break is a percentage discount (a
  **Percent** price change with quantity breaks, such as −10% from 10 and
  −15% from 25).
- *Add 50 more for $8.90 each*, for any other break: the price per item at
  the next break, for the same choices.

The hint looks at the base price's quantity breaks and the quantity breaks on
every price change in play, steps onto your quantity grid, and stays quiet
when the next break is beyond the maximum or doesn't lower the price per item.
It is a display only, and changes nothing about the price charged.

## Quantity per option (size runs)

A bulk T-shirt order is one design printed on a mix of sizes. Without a
count per size, the customer adds each size as a separate cart line, and five
M plus three L plus two 3XL are priced as three small jobs instead of one
order of ten.

A **Quantity per option** field gives each option its own stepper. The
**total** of the counts is the job's quantity: it drives the base price per
unit, quantity breaks, formulas and the cart.

<Note>
    Quantity per option is available on Shopify, and on WooCommerce from
    plugin version 0.4.16.
</Note>

### Set it up

1.  **Add the field.** Add a field of type **Quantity per option**. It
    starts as *Sizes* with S, M, L and XL; rename and add options on the
    **Options** tab (XS to 4XL, say).
2.  **Choose the display.** **Grid of steppers** shows a card per option;
    **List** shows one row per option.
3.  **Set the limits** on the **Basics** tab:

    | Setting | What it does |
    | --- | --- |
    | **Min per option** | The smallest count for an option, when that option is ordered at all. |
    | **Max per option** | The largest count for one option. Empty means no limit. |
    | **Step** | Counts go up in this step, per option. |
    | **Minimum total** | The smallest order across all options. |
    | **Maximum total** | The largest order. Empty means no limit. |
    | **Unit** | Shown after the total: *Total: 10 shirts*. |
    | **Show 'add N more' hint** | The [next price break](#show-the-next-price-break) hint, counted on the total. |

4.  **Price it.** Set the base price to **Per unit × quantity** (the
    price of one shirt). Then:
    - For a surcharge on some sizes, open that option's details and add a
      **Per item of this option** price change: a 3XL at +€3 adds €3 for
      each 3XL ordered.
    - For a volume discount on the whole order, add a **Percent** price
      change on the field's own **Pricing** tab with quantity breaks, such
      as 0% up to 9, −10% up to 24, −15% up to 49 and −20% above.
5.  **Publish**, and remove your platform's own size variants from the
    product: the grid replaces them.

<Tip>
    An option can carry **Per item of this option**, per page, per area, per
    length, **Fixed** and **Setup** prices. Fixed and setup prices are charged
    once if that option is ordered at all, for example a different blank for
    4XL. Percentages, multipliers and formulas act on the whole order, so they
    belong on the field instead, and the builder doesn't offer them on an
    option.
</Tip>

### Example

Base price €19.90 per unit, 3XL +€3 per item, and the volume discount
above. An order of M × 5, L × 3 and 3XL × 2 is ten shirts:

| | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Base: 10 shirts × €19.90 | €199.00 |
| 3XL: 2 × €3 | €6.00 |
| Volume discount, −10% | −€20.50 |
| **Total** | **€184.50** |

With the hint on, the summary also says *Add 15 more to get 15% off*.

### What the customer sees

A stepper for each size and a running *Total: 10 shirts*. If the order is
below the minimum total, the field asks them to *Choose at least 10 in total*
(or *at most*, above the maximum), and add to cart stops at the field until
the order fits. On the cart line and the order, the field reads like this:

```text
Size: M × 5, L × 3, 3XL × 2
```

Sizes are listed in your option order and sizes with no count are left out.
See [Cart & orders](/print-options/guides/cart-and-orders) for the rest of the
line.

<Note>
    Option ids on a quantity per option field may only use letters, digits,
    `-` and `_`. Ids made by the builder always do; the builder flags any
    other.
</Note>

## Quantity fields that take turns

Some products are sold in different quantities depending on an earlier
choice: one paper comes in packs of 100, 250 and 500, another only in 500 and
1000. Give each group its own quantity field, and let them take turns:

1.  Add a **Quantity** field for each paper group, each with its own presets.
2.  On each one's **Logic** tab, add a visibility rule on the paper field, so
    exactly one quantity field shows for any paper.
3.  Publish.

The quantity field that is showing is the quantity. At most one quantity
field may show without a visibility rule; with two, the builder says *Only one
quantity field can show unconditionally* and asks you to give the others a
rule. A quantity field's own rule can't depend on the quantity.
