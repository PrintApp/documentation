When a customer adds a configured job to the cart, Print Options recalculates
the price on our servers, and the job arrives as a cart line that carries the
whole specification. This page covers what that line contains and the choices
you have about it.

## What the cart line shows

A configured job is one cart line. It carries:

- **The price our servers calculated** for the job. The total the customer saw
  in the configurator is a preview; this is the price charged. See
  [Prices are verified, always](/print-options/guides/pricing#prices-are-verified-always).
- **Every answer, under your own labels**, such as *Paper: 350gsm matte*,
  *Size: 24 × 18 in* and *Quantity: 500 cards* (with the quantity field's
  [unit](/print-options/guides/quantities#the-quantity-field) word after the
  number). A [quantity per option](/print-options/guides/quantities#quantity-per-option-size-runs)
  field lists its counts: *Size: M × 5, L × 3, 3XL × 2*. An uploaded file is
  listed by its file name.
- **The resolved SKU**, combined from the SKU fragments on the chosen options.
- **On Shopify, the copies in the title**: a job of 500 business cards is
  titled *Business Cards × 500*, so the order list shows the size of each job
  at a glance. WooCommerce keeps the product's own title.

The same details are stored on the order line, so your production team reads
them from the order, not from the customer's email.

### How fields are named

Each answer is listed under the field's label, so give every field a label:
that is how it is named on the order. A field without a label is named, in
this order of preference:

1.  after its **section title**, when it is the only question in its section
    (a *Size* section holding one unlabeled size question reads *Size: 24 × 18
    in*),
2.  *Quantity* (in the customer's language), for a quantity field,
3.  its internal id, as a last resort.

Info / summary fields are not questions and are left out.

<Tip>
    On Shopify, line-item properties that other apps or your theme add to the
    product form, such as a gift note or a design reference from the
    Print.App designer, are kept on the configured job's cart line alongside
    the options.
</Tip>

## The cart quantity

By default a configured job is **one line with quantity 1**: the line's price
is the whole job, and the copies are shown in its title and options. That
keeps every job, however many copies, in one tidy line.

If you want your store's quantity column, stock and shipping weight to use the
real number of copies, open **Product** settings in the builder and, under
**Cart quantity**, change **Line quantity**:

| Line quantity | The cart line |
| --- | --- |
| **One line per job (quantity 1)** | Quantity 1, at the job's total. The default. |
| **Real quantity (when the price divides evenly)** | Quantity = the copies, at the price per copy. |

Real quantity only applies when the verified total divides into equal prices
per copy, to the cent (to your store's price decimals on WooCommerce). A job
of 250 flyers at $12.00 would be 250 × $0.048, which a cart can't charge, so
that job stays one line at the same total. The customer never pays a different
amount either way. On Shopify, real quantity
also needs the customer to pay in your store's default currency, and allows at
most 2,000 copies per line, Shopify's own limit for a bundle component.

<Note>
    On WooCommerce, a real-quantity line's quantity is fixed at the number of
    copies, in the classic cart and the cart and checkout blocks alike: the
    copies are part of the configuration and its verified price. To order a
    different number, the customer changes it on the product page, where the
    price is verified again.
</Note>

<Warning>
    With real quantity, every copy counts against the product's stock. If you
    track stock on the product, make sure it covers your largest jobs before
    you switch.
</Warning>

## Shipping weight on WooCommerce

Set a product's **Weight** in WooCommerce to the weight of **one copy**. A
configured job of 500 cards then ships at 500 × that weight, whichever cart
quantity mode you use, so shipping rates that depend on weight are right.

Developers can adjust the weight with the `papo_cart_item_weight` filter,
which receives the line's total weight, the weight of one copy, the number of
copies and the cart item.

## Downloading customer uploads

Files customers upload through a **File upload** field (shown as a
**Dropzone**) come with a download link on the order:

- **WooCommerce:** open the order in your admin. Each line with an uploaded
  file has a **Download file** button under it. It works for older orders
  too, as long as the file is still stored.
- **Shopify:** open the order. The line item's properties include **Download
  file** (in the customer's language), right under the file name. It is an
  ordinary line property, so depending on your theme the customer may see it
  in the cart too, where it leads to the file they uploaded.

Each click downloads the file under the name the customer gave it. Files are
kept for **30 days** after upload, then removed automatically; after that the
link shows *File no longer available*. Download what you need into your
production workflow within that time.

<Note>
    The link needs no login: its long random address is what protects it, like
    a private sharing link. Treat it accordingly, and share it only with the
    people who produce the job.
</Note>

Files checked by [Filecheck](/print-options/guides/uploads#filecheck-validated-uploads)
and designs made in the Print.App designer don't get this link: you reach
them through Filecheck and Print.App, where they are stored.

<Tip>
    WooCommerce developers can change the link with the
    `papo_file_download_url` filter, which receives the URL and the file id.
</Tip>
