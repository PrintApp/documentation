Print Options prices the way print shops quote: a base, adjustments for the
choices made, rates that scale with pages, area or length, then fees and
floors. When your price follows a curve that none of these describe, a
[formula](/print-options/guides/formula-pricing) can calculate it directly.

## The base price

Every option set has a base price, set under **Product** settings:

- **Once per order** — a flat starting amount for the job.
- **Per unit × quantity** — multiplied by the quantity the customer picks.
  This is the usual choice for cards, flyers and stickers.

A base price of 0 is left out of the customer's price summary, so a product
priced entirely by its options doesn't show a "Base price $0.00" line.

<Note>
    If your option set has a quantity field but nothing in it depends on the
    quantity, the builder warns: *Changing the quantity does not change the
    price.* Two items then cost the same as one. Set the base price to **Per
    unit × quantity**, or price the options **Per unit** instead of
    **Fixed**.
</Note>

## Price changes on choices

Each choice can carry one or more price changes. Select a choice in the
builder, open its details, and add them under **Pricing**. A field can carry
price changes too, on its own **Pricing** tab: they apply whenever the field
is shown, whatever the customer chooses in it.

| Type | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Fixed** | Added once, regardless of quantity. Good for a plate or die charge. |
| **Per unit** | Multiplied by the quantity. |
| **Per page** | Multiplied by the uploaded file's page count and the quantity. |
| **Per colour page** / **Per mono page** | The same, split by colour and black-and-white pages. Needs a file source that reports them, such as [Filecheck](/print-options/guides/uploads). |
| **Per area** | Multiplied by the job's area and quantity — for banners, posters and stickers. |
| **Per length** | Multiplied by the job's length and quantity — for roll media and gang sheets. See [Length pricing](#length-pricing). |
| **Percent** | A percentage of the running total. Good for premium finishes. |
| **Multiplier** | Multiplies the running total, e.g. `1.1` for double-sided. |
| **Setup** | A flat fee added at the very end, after percentages and multipliers. |
| **Formula** | The price worked out from the quantity, size and page count. See [Pricing by formula](/print-options/guides/formula-pricing). |

<Note>
    Order matters, and it is fixed: amounts (formulas included) first, then
    percentages, then multipliers, then setup fees, then the minimum price.
    That is why a paper upgrade multiplier never inflates your plate fee.
</Note>

<Tip>
    On a [quantity per option](/print-options/guides/quantities#quantity-per-option-size-runs)
    field, each option's **Per item of this option** price is multiplied by
    that option's own count: a 3XL surcharge is charged on the 3XL shirts
    only.
</Tip>

## Quantity breaks

Any per-unit, per-page or per-area price — including the base price — can use a
tier table instead of a single amount. Add tiers in the builder and give each
row an upper bound, leaving the last row open-ended.

Two modes:

- **Flat** — the whole quantity is priced at the matched row's rate. 500 cards
  at the "up to 999" rate means all 500 use that rate.
- **Graduated** — each band is priced at its own rate and summed, like income
  tax brackets.

```text Example: flat tiers on a per-unit base price
up to 249    $0.08 each
up to 499    $0.06 each
up to 999    $0.045 each
thereafter   $0.035 each
```

For the base price, click **Add quantity breaks** under **Product** settings.
While breaks are set, the base price **Amount** is not used: the rows are the
price. **Remove breaks** goes back to a single amount, starting from the first
row's.

The configurator can tell customers how close the next break is ("Add 15 more
to get 15% off"): see
[Show the next price break](/print-options/guides/quantities#show-the-next-price-break).

## Price matrix: an exact price per combination

When your supplier quotes a price for every combination (this paper, these
sides, this turnaround, this finish, this quantity), adjusting a base price is
the wrong tool. Use the **price matrix** under **Product** settings instead:
every combination of your choice fields gets its own exact price.

1. Make each option a **choice field**, quantity included: offer the
   quantities your supplier prices (100, 250, 500…) as choices.
2. Leave those options without price changes of their own, and set the base
   price to **0, once per order**. A matrix price then is exactly what the
   customer pays.
3. Under **Price matrix**, add the options one by one. The builder lists
   every combination.
4. Fill in the prices. One or two options show as a grid you can paste a
   spreadsheet block into; any number show as a list, one row per combination,
   with filters to find a row.

Untick a combination your supplier does not offer: the configurator marks the
choices that lead to it as unavailable, and it cannot be ordered. Leave a
price blank to use the base price for that combination.

Only **Single choice** fields can be matrix options. The builder lists your
other fields under the matrix with a **Make single choice** button, which
changes that field's type for you. If you later delete a field the matrix
uses, or change its type, the builder drops it from the matrix so the option
set stays valid.

There is no fixed limit on the number of options or combinations. An option
set can be up to 1 MB, and price matrices are stored compactly, so a price
list across six or seven options with a few thousand combinations fits.

### Import a price list

**Import / export CSV** shows every combination as a table: a column per
option, then **Price**, **Available** and **SKU**. Download it (or copy it),
fill in the prices in your spreadsheet, and choose the file again, or paste
the rows straight from the spreadsheet into the box, then click **Apply
prices**.

- Options and choices are matched by name, so the columns must be named
  after your options and the cells must use your choice names.
- Commas, semicolons and tabs all work as separators, and prices such as
  `12,50` or `€ 1.234,50` are read correctly.
- A row that cannot be matched is skipped and listed with the reason. If a
  whole column is missing, nothing is changed.

<Note>
    A matrix price replaces the base price, so it follows the base price's
    setting: once per order, or per unit × quantity. Price changes on the
    matrix's own choices are still added on top, and base-price quantity
    breaks take precedence over the matrix. The builder warns you about both.
</Note>

## Area pricing

For products sized by the customer, add a **Dimensions** field and give the
relevant choice a **Per area** price change. Set the unit it is priced in —
square metres, square feet or square inches — and the engine converts the
customer's width × height for you.

<Tip>
    If your product's size comes from the uploaded artwork rather than a
    dimensions field, leave the field reference empty: the area is then taken
    from the file's own canvas size.
</Tip>

A per-area price is the same rate for every size and quantity. If small items
need a minimum price, or the rate changes with the size *and* the quantity,
use a [formula](/print-options/guides/formula-pricing) instead.

## Length pricing

Roll media, banners by the metre and DTF gang sheets are priced by length:
the film or roll has a fixed width, and the customer pays for the length they
use. Give the field or choice a **Per length** price change and set:

- **The unit it is priced in:** per m, per ft, per in or per cm.
- **Where the size comes from:** a dimensions field, or **From uploaded file
  canvas** to measure the artwork itself (a gang sheet editor's sheet, for
  instance).
- **Which side is the length:** **Height (length)**, the default for a roll
  whose width is fixed, **Width**, or **Longest side**.

Length rates can carry breaks on the length, so long runs get cheaper per
metre, or on the quantity.

## Order rules

Under **Product** settings you will find two whole-order rules:

- **Setup fee** — added once to every order, after all percentages and
  multipliers.
- **Minimum price** — the total never drops below this, however the options
  are configured.

## Prices are verified, always

The total in the configurator is a live preview. When the customer adds the job
to the cart, the same pricing rules run again **on our servers** and the result
is signed. Your store charges that verified price.

This means a customer who edits prices in their browser achieves nothing — the
add to cart is simply refused. You never have to reconcile a browser price with
what you meant to charge.
