---
title: Cut Contour Lines
description: "Show the cut line on the canvas and output it as a named spot color your cutting plotter can read"
---

Round stickers, shaped labels, phone cases and other die-cut products are cut along an outline, not along a rectangle. Print.App can show that outline to your customers in the editor and write it into the print-ready PDF as a **vector stroke in a named spot color** (for example `CutContour`), which is what cutting plotters and RIPs look for.

<Note>
This applies to **die-cut pages**: pages clipped to a custom shape, like the round and shaped templates in the PrintFX editor's Stickers category. Rectangular pages are cut at the trim box, and keep using the regular trim marks (**Render Marks** in your PDF Output Profile).
</Note>

## Show the cut line on the canvas

In the editor, open the **Design** panel and switch on **Show Canvas Size**. Besides the width and height dimension lines, a die-cut page then gets a dashed magenta line **along its edge**, in the same style as the dashed **Text Safety Margin**. On a round sticker the customer sees the circle where the cut happens, not a square around it.

**Show Canvas Boundary** draws the same edge line on die-cut pages. On rectangular pages it keeps drawing the dashed rectangle.

These lines are editor guides only. They never appear in previews, thumbnails or the PDF.

## Output the cut line in the PDF

The cut line is controlled by the [PDF Output Profile](/guides/projects/pdf-output-profiles) assigned to the design.

1. Open your PDF Output Profiles and create a profile, or clone an existing one. The store default profiles cannot be edited.
2. Under **Dieline**, switch on **Render Dieline**.
3. Set **Dieline Spot Name** to the separation name your cutter expects. `CutContour` is the default; other common names are `Thru-cut`, `Kiss-cut` and `CutContour1`. The name must match exactly, including capitalization.
4. Optionally set **Dieline Width** (in points). The default is `0.25 pt`, a hairline.
5. Save the profile and [assign it to your design template](/guides/projects/pdf-output-profiles#assigning-pdf-output-profiles-to-design-templates).

Every die-cut page rendered with that profile then gets:

- the artwork clipped to the shape,
- the shape's outline drawn on top as a stroke-only vector path,
- the stroke in the named spot color, with a 100% magenta CMYK alternate so it is visible in any viewer,
- **overprint** on the stroke, so it does not knock out the artwork underneath.

<Tip>
The cut line stays a named spot color even when the profile has **Render Spot Colors** switched off or uses **Device RGB**. Those settings affect your artwork's colors, not the cut line, because the plotter finds the line by its name.
</Tip>

## Check the output

Open the PDF in Acrobat (**Print Production → Output Preview**) or your RIP. The spot color you named should appear as its own separation, containing only the cut outline.
