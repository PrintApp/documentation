Data Sources let a design fill itself in. A customer picks their office, and the office's address, phone number and VAT number appear on the business card. A customer ticks "Contains nuts", and the allergen warning appears on the label.

You set this up in the PrintFx editor, when you open a design from your dashboard. Customers only see the result: a form with the questions you chose, and a design that fills in as they answer.

There are three parts:

| Part | What it is | Where you set it up |
|---|---|---|
| **Tables** | Spreadsheets (CSV) you upload, such as a list of offices | Your dashboard: **Designs → More actions → Data Sources** |
| **Fields** | Named values on a design: what the customer enters, picks, or what is looked up or worked out | The editor: **Design** tab → **Data Sources** |
| **Rules** | When an item shows | The editor: select the item, then **Object → Data → Shows** |

## Upload a table

1. In your dashboard, go to **Designs → More actions → Data Sources**.
2. Click **Upload CSV** and pick the file.
3. The dashboard says how many rows it uploaded, or what's wrong with the file.

The first row of the file holds the column names. Each row after it is one record:

```text
Office,Address,Phone
Copenhagen,"Vesterbrogade 1, 2nd floor",+45 33 12 34 56
Amsterdam,Damrak 1,+31 20 123 4567
```

- **The first column is the key**: the column lookups search unless you choose another. Keep its values unique.
- **Designs name the table by its file name**, without `.csv`: `Offices.csv` is `Offices`.
- **To change a table, upload the new file with the same name.** It replaces the old one, and every design that uses it reads the new rows.
- **Limits:** 5,000 rows, 50 columns, and 350 KB. For a bigger list, split it into several files, or remove columns no design uses.
- Save Excel files as **CSV (UTF-8)** first, so accents and symbols come through.

The same tables also make [Design Variants](/guides/designs/variations/variation-datasource): one version of a design per row.

## The Data Sources section

Open a design and go to the **Design** tab. The **Data Sources** section sums up what the design uses, for example "4 fields · 1 table · 1 rule". Click it to see the list:

- **1 · Tables**: the tables the design can read. Click one to see its first rows and the fields that read it. If you uploaded a table while the editor was open, click **Refresh**.
- **2 · Fields**: each field and where its value comes from, in the order they're filled. Click one to open it, or click **Add a field**.
- **3 · Rules**: the items that only show sometimes, and when.

A field shown in amber needs a look. For example, it might be used in two ways that don't fit together.

## Fields

A field is one named value, such as `Office` or `Office address`. Fields are made for you when you make a text something the customer fills in (see [Smart Objects](/guides/designs/objects/smart)), or you can add one under **Fields → Add a field**.

Open a field and choose **Where its value comes from**:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Customer enters it">
    The customer types it in the form, or ticks a box. Under **Input**, choose the kind of value: Text, Email, Phone, Link, Number, Money, Percentage, Date, or Yes / No (a tick box). The editor then checks what the customer types and writes it in the format you set, for example a date as 21 May 2027 or a phone number with its country code.

    **Starts with** is the value the design shows before the customer changes it, and **Required** makes the customer fill it in.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Customer picks">
    The customer picks one of a list. Under **Choices**, take them from **A table column** (choose the table and column) or from **A list I type** (one choice per line).

    **Starts on** chooses the option the design opens with, so it never shows blank. Pick **Customer chooses** to start with nothing chosen.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="From a table">
    The value is looked up in one of your tables, and the customer doesn't see it in the form. Under **Look it up**, choose:

    - **Table**: which table, for example Offices;
    - **Find the row where**: the column to search, and **Equals field**: the field whose value it must match, for example the Office the customer picked;
    - **Column**: the column whose value to use, for example Address;
    - **No match**: what to show when no row matches. Pick **Leave it blank**, or type a fallback.

    **Try it** runs the lookup on a real row, so you can check it before saving. Matching ignores upper and lower case and spaces at either end.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Worked out">
    The value is worked out from other fields with a [formula](#formulas), for example a best-before date 18 months after the packing date. The customer doesn't see it in the form. Use **Insert field** to add a field to the formula where the cursor is. Under the formula, the panel shows the result for sample values, or what's wrong with it.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

Two kinds of field hold something other than text:

- **A picture field**: the customer gives a picture, and every frame that uses the field shows it.
- **A colour field**: the customer picks a colour, and every item that uses the field is painted with it. List the colours to offer, separated by `;`, as `#hex` codes or names from your palettes.

**Used by**, at the bottom of a field's panel, lists every text, picture, colour and rule that reads the field. Click one to select it on the design.

## Connect an item to data

Select an item on the design, then go to the **Object** tab. Its **Data** section has three lines. Click one to change it.

**Content** is what the item shows:

- For a text: **Fixed**, or **Customer fills it in**. To fill only part of a text, type the field's name in curly brackets inside the text, for example `Tel. {Phone}`.
- For a picture frame:
  - **Fixed picture**;
  - **Customer replaces it**;
  - **Picked by a field**: a field's value chooses the picture. Choose the **Field** and where the pictures come **From**, either the frame's own graphics or one of your graphics folders. The frame shows the picture named like the value, so *Salted caramel* picks `salted-caramel.png`.

**Colour** is where the item's colour comes from: **Fixed** (the colour you gave it), or **A colour field**, whose value paints it.

**Shows** decides when the item is on the design:
- **Always**;
- **Only when…**: choose a **Field**, a **Condition** (*is*, *isn't*, *is empty*, *isn't empty*; for a tick box, *is ticked* or *isn't ticked*) and the **Value** to compare with.

A hidden item is left out of the preview and the print file.

## Long values

A value from a table or a formula can be longer than the space you left for it, for example a long address. Select the text and turn on **Typography → Shrink to Fit**:

- the text box keeps its size;
- the font shrinks until each line fits, down to **Min font size** (half the starting size unless you change it);
- it fits again every time the value changes.

If a value is too long even at the minimum size, the panel says "Too long to fit, even at the minimum size".

Each paragraph stays on one line, so put a line break wherever the text may break.

## Formulas

**Worked out** fields, **Advanced** lookups and **Only when…** rules are formulas. They are written like spreadsheet formulas:

| Write | For |
|---|---|
| `[Office]` | the value of a field (its name in square brackets) |
| `"Head office"` | words (in straight double quotes) |
| `12`, `2.5` | numbers |
| `&` | joining text: `[First name] & " " & [Last name]` |
| `+ - * /` | sums |
| `= <> < > <= >=` | comparing; `<>` is "isn't" |
| `and`, `or`, `not` | combining conditions |
| `is empty`, `is not empty` | whether a field has a value |

Some useful functions:

| Formula | Gives |
|---|---|
| `lookup("Offices", [Office], "Address")` | the Address on the row whose first column matches the Office field |
| `lookup_or("Offices", [Office], "Fax", "")` | the same, with a fallback when no row matches or the cell is empty |
| `if([Plan] = "Pro", "Pro member", "")` | one value or another, by a condition |
| `upper([Name])`, `lower()`, `proper()`, `trim()` | changed case, trimmed spaces |
| `date_add([Packed on], 18, "months")` | a date 18 months later (also `"days"`, `"weeks"`, `"years"`) |
| `format_date([Best before], "D MMM YYYY")` | a date written out, for example 21 May 2027 |
| `format_currency([Price], "EUR")` | an amount with its currency |
| `match([Flavour], "Flavours")` | the picture (or colour) named like the Flavour field, from the Flavours graphics folder (or palette) |

The full list: `abs`, `ceil`, `coalesce`, `concat`, `contains`, `date`, `date_add`, `day`, `days_between`, `ean13_check`, `ends_with`, `floor`, `format_currency`, `format_date`, `format_number`, `format_percent`, `format_phone`, `gs1_check`, `gs1_link`, `if`, `iferror`, `ifs`, `isempty`, `iserror`, `left`, `len`, `lookup`, `lookup_or`, `lower`, `match`, `max`, `mecard`, `mid`, `min`, `month`, `pad_left`, `pad_right`, `parse_date`, `parse_number`, `proper`, `repeat`, `replace`, `right`, `round`, `split_part`, `starts_with`, `sum`, `switch`, `text`, `trim`, `upc_check`, `upper`, `url_with_utm`, `value`, `vcard`, `weekday`, `wifi`, `year`.

## Example: an office on a business card

1. **Upload `Offices.csv`**, with the columns `Office`, `Address` and `Phone`.
2. **Add a field `Office`**: **Customer picks**, with **Choices** from **A table column**: table *Offices*, column *Office*.
3. **Add a field `Office address`**: **From a table**. Table *Offices*, find the row where *Office* equals field *Office*, column *Address*, no match: *Leave it blank*. **Try it** shows an address.
4. **Add a field `Office phone`** the same way, with the column *Phone*.
5. **Put the fields on the card:** in a text, type `{Office address}`, and in another `Tel. {Office phone}`. Select the address text and turn on **Shrink to Fit**.
6. **Save, and try it as a customer.** The form asks only for the office, and the address and phone fill in.

When the address list changes, upload `Offices.csv` again: the card reads the new addresses, with no change to the design.
